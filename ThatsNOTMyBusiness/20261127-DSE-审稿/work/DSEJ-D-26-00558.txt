Abstract:
Multi-hop question answering benchmarks are essential for evaluating complex
reasoning in large language models, yet static datasets degrade over time as real-
world facts evolve and advancing models saturate questions through memorization.
Conventional curation treats verification merely as a terminal discard filter, which
steadily shrinks evaluation coverage without preventing obsolescence because flat
representations cannot preserve intermediate proof artifacts. To overcome this
limitation, we present EverGreen, a framework that introduces persistent Evidence
Graphs to sustain benchmarks across a closed loop of audit, repair, and evolution.
Each Evidence Graph models atomic claims, causal dependencies, and timestamped
web evidence, enabling systematic auditing in topological dependency order across
factual, compositional, and uniqueness dimensions. Across MuSiQue, HotpotQA, and
2WikiMultiHopQA, EverGreen reveals that up to 19.3% of validation answers are
incorrect and 27.7% of audited answer spaces are non-unique, flaws that distort
relative model rankings. Compared with human expert verification, automated auditing
achieves comparable accuracy while delivering a nearly sixfold increase in throughput.
Guided by verified Evidence Graphs, backward repair restores 82.8% of confirmed
defects by editing an average of only 1.29 claim nodes per item, while forward
evolution mutates graph topologies to synthesize robust descendant questions that
resist parametric memorization. Grounding benchmark governance in Evidence
Graphs thus enables evaluation suites to be continually corrected and extended rather
than depleted.

Abstract
Multi-hop question answering benchmarks are essential for evaluating complex reasoning in large
language models, yet static datasets degrade over time as real-world facts evolve and advancing
models saturate questions through memorization. Conventional curation treats verification merely as
a terminal discard filter, which steadily shrinks evaluation coverage without preventing obsolescence
because flat representations cannot preserve intermediate proof artifacts. To overcome this limitation,
we present EverGreen, a framework that introduces persistent Evidence Graphs to sustain benchmarks
across a closed loop of audit, repair, and evolution. Each Evidence Graph models atomic claims, causal
dependencies, and timestamped web evidence, enabling systematic auditing in topological dependency
order across factual, compositional, and uniqueness dimensions. Across MuSiQue, HotpotQA, and
2WikiMultiHopQA, EverGreen reveals that up to 19.3% of validation answers are incorrect and
27.7% of audited answer spaces are non-unique, flaws that distort relative model rankings. Compared
with human expert verification, automated auditing achieves comparable accuracy while delivering a
nearly sixfold increase in throughput. Guided by verified Evidence Graphs, backward repair restores
82.8% of confirmed defects by editing an average of only 1.29 claim nodes per item, while forward
evolution mutates graph topologies to synthesize robust descendant questions that resist parametric
memorization. Grounding benchmark governance in Evidence Graphs thus enables evaluation suites
to be continually corrected and extended rather than depleted.

1 Introduction
Multi-hop question answering (QA) benchmarks
are central to evaluating complex reasoning in
large language models (LLMs), measuring multi-
step evidence integration, ranking frontier archi-
tectures, and providing testbeds for retrieval-
augmented systems [2, 5, 8, 16, 30]. A benchmark
item is foundational evaluation infrastructure, yet
existing datasets are released as static question-
answer pairs whose correctness is asserted once
and rarely revisited. Because multi-hop reason-
ing requires cascading transitions across heteroge-
neous sources, a single stale premise, misaligned
entity binding, or unconstrained phrasing is suffi-
cient to invalidate an entire evaluation item.
Scaling and reusing these benchmarks reveals
that static items erode along two independent
clocks, namely a factual clock governed by real-
world change and a model clock driven by rapid
capability advances. Under the factual clock,
real-world facts evolve, rendering originally cor-
rect answers obsolete. Simultaneously, manual

tion, EverGreen decouples graph mutation from
question-surface projection and enforces an adver-
sarial boundary-stop control law, which contin-
ues structural hardening until an independent
examiner model fails rather than stopping at an
arbitrary computational budget.

2 Related Work
2.1 Annotation Quality
Multi-hop question answering requires synthesiz-
ing scattered evidence across multiple sources and
performing compositional reasoning over interme-
diate steps [2, 16, 17]. Widely used benchmarks
such as HotpotQA [36], 2WikiMultiHopQA [9],
and MuSiQue [30] evaluate this capability by pro-
viding multi-document contexts alongside anno-
tated supporting paragraphs. However, construc-
tion pipelines relying on automatic entity linking,
text scraping, and paragraph concatenation intro-
duce systemic vulnerabilities. Models frequently
bypass multi-step reasoning by exploiting lexical
overlap, entity type hints, and single-hop short-
cuts [11, 22]. Beyond heuristic shortcuts, anno-
tation errors remain widespread even in estab-
lished corpora [24]. Open-world question formu-
lations also frequently omit temporal boundaries
or geographic qualifiers, creating underspecified
queries with multiple valid answers [28]. As real-
world facts evolve, static gold annotations rapidly
become obsolete [29, 31].
These annotation flaws undermine the relia-
bility of empirical evaluations and leaderboards.
Recent studies report pervasive noise across adja-
cent data tasks, showing that over 30% of exam-
ples in standard benchmarks contain incorrect
annotations and that correcting these errors sub-
stantially alters model rankings [13]. Manual
audits of widely reused corpora reach the same
conclusion beyond QA, uncovering systematic
quality defects that large-scale collection pipelines
never filter [15]. Cheng et al. [3] emphasize
that noisy reference answers mask genuine model
progress and produce fragile performance conclu-
sions. Traditional benchmark curation typically

2.2 Automated Verification
Automated verification has evolved from binary
classification of isolated claims to structured
evidence retrieval and fine-grained error local-
ization.
Decontextualization
and
atomic
fact
decomposition frameworks break complex model
outputs into individual propositions that can
be independently verified against retrieval cor-
puses [7, 20, 23]. Search-augmented evaluators
scale this paradigm to long-form answers by issu-
ing grounded queries for each atomic fact [34],
while sampling-consistency checks and planned
verification chains detect hallucinations without
consulting external references [4, 21]. Program-
guided approaches further translate verification
into executable sub-tasks to improve interpretabil-
ity and retrieval precision [25]. Similarly, auto-
mated evaluation systems such as ARES [26]
assess retrieval-augmented generation [8] by mea-
suring context relevance and answer faithfulness
against retrieved documents.
Deploying large language models directly as
evaluators has gained widespread popularity [38],
but ungrounded model judges often exhibit sys-
tematic position bias, verbosity preferences, and
self-enhancement tendencies [19, 32, 33]. Although
graph-structured evidence has proven effective
for multi-hop retrieval and corpus-level reason-
ing [6, 12], prevailing verification frameworks
predominantly operate over flat lists of inde-
pendent claims. As a consequence, they verify
factual correctness in isolation, leaving compo-
sitional entailment between reasoning hops and
answer-space uniqueness unexamined. EverGreen
structures multi-hop verification as an auditable
dependency graph over factual, compositional,
and uniqueness dimensions, anchoring intermedi-
ate decisions in timestamped web evidence.
2.3 Dynamic Benchmarking
Rapid advances in model pre-training and reason-
ing architectures have accelerated the saturation
of static benchmarks, while test set contami-
nation further erodes evaluation integrity [27,
31, 37]. To maintain evaluation utility, recent
research has explored dynamic evaluation frame-
works that continuously refresh test instances,
ranging from human-in-the-loop adversarial col-
lection [14] to periodically rebuilt contamination-
limited suites [35]. However, existing dynamic
benchmarks predominantly depend on manual
crowdsourcing or unconstrained large language
model generation. Human authoring requires pro-
hibitive manual effort and proves difficult to
standardize. Conversely, unconstrained generation
risks compounding hallucinations, broken logic
chains, and noisy reference answers.
A parallel line of work uses heuristic expan-
sions
to
synthesize
multi-hop
questions,
but
increasing question length or hop count does
not automatically guarantee reasoning difficulty.
Unconstrained synthetic questions often collapse
into
shallow
pattern
matching
or
single-hop
retrieval shortcuts because raw generation length
does not reflect intrinsic reasoning complexity.
EverGreen addresses these limitations by repur-
posing verification artifacts into a generative sub-
strate. By applying graph mutation operators to
verified subgraphs and enforcing an adversarial
boundary-stop condition where generation contin-
ues until an independent model examiner fails,
EverGreen derives challenging descendant ques-
tions that preserve strict factual provenance and
resist parametric memory.

3.2 Diagnostic Auditing
Topological Scheduling. EverGreen schedules
claim verification in topological dependency order
(formalized in Algorithm 1) to ensure that inter-
mediate premises are validated prior to evalu-
ating downstream assertions. A claim node c
is designated as ready for evaluation when all
its immediate prerequisites possess non-tentative
truth assignments, satisfying
∀d ∈deps(c),

Evaluating ready claims in ascending topological
order establishes deterministic evidence propaga-
tion and avoids redundant retrieval. If any prereq-
uisite claim d ∈deps(c) is evaluated as Refuted
without a verified alternative hypothesis, Ever-
Green marks dependent claim c as Refuted
and prunes downstream retrieval branches (Algo-
rithm 1). This deterministic short-circuiting saves
substantial compute and search overhead, cutting
redundant model and retrieval actions by 34.2%
as evaluated in Section 4.6.
To verify a ready fact claim, the verifica-
tion agent queries external search APIs under an
instance-level action budget B, retrieving relevant
pages and extracting verbatim candidate passages.
Claim states update through a multi-source cor-
roboration protocol, wherein a claim transitions to
Supported or Refuted when corroborated by
at least two independent web sources from distinct
registrable domains, or when anchored by author-
itative primary references such as official gov-
ernmental portals and established encyclopedias.
Contradictory evidence across sources or failure
to locate corroborating citations within budget B
assigns the claim to Ambiguous. A refuted key
claim generates a candidate alternative c′ satisfy-
ing s(c′) = Supported through localized search,
establishing an alternative of (c′, c) relation that
restores dependency viability.

4.1 Experimental Setup
To evaluate EverGreen across diverse bench-
mark construction methodologies, we conduct
experiments on three representative multi-hop
QA corpora: HotpotQA [36], 2WikiMultiHopQA
(2WikiMHQA) [9], and MuSiQue [30]. These
datasets reflect distinct authoring paradigms,
spanning Wikipedia paragraph linking, Wikidata
relation paths, and disconnected sub-question
composition. For MuSiQue, we audit both the
complete validation split (2,415 instances) and
the full training split (19,902 instances); for Hot-
potQA and 2WikiMHQA, we evaluate standard
validation subsets of 988 and 989 instances follow-
ing HippoRAG [12]. Based on our audit, we estab-
lish cleaned evaluation subsets (Dclean) by filtering
unrepairable instances while incorporating verified
label corrections and expanded alias answer sets
A∗, yielding Nclean = 1,872 for MuSiQue, 884 for
HotpotQA, and 962 for 2WikiMHQA.
We employ DeepSeek-Flash as the backbone
verification model and use the Jina search API
for web retrieval, anchoring each factual deci-
sion to timestamped external sources. To measure
how annotation errors affect model assessment, we
evaluate a panel of six models across four archi-
tectural families and parameter scales from 8B to
235B (Qwen3-8B, Qwen3-235B-A22B, Llama 3.1
8B, Llama 3.3 70B, DeepSeek-Flash, and GPT-5-
mini), using greedy decoding (T = 0). On original
benchmarks, predictions are scored against pub-
lished gold answers via exact match and standard
alias lookup; on cleaned subsets, model outputs
are matched against verified answer sets A∗under
standard text normalization, using Qwen-plus as
an equivalence judge to accommodate benign sur-
face variations.
Subsequent lifecycle experiments investigate
backward maintenance and forward evolution,
whose
task-specific
cohorts,
agent
configura-
tions, and evaluation protocols are detailed in
Sections 4.4 and 4.5, respectively.

4.4 Backward Maintenance via
Minimum-Cost Repair
Identifying benchmark decay is only an ini-
tial step; sustaining long-term evaluation utility
requires principled mechanisms to repair flawed
instances rather than discarding them. Conven-
tional curation typically resorts to outright fil-
tering, which would discard up to 30.0% of the
MuSiQue validation split and disproportionately
purge its most challenging compositional ques-
tions. Structured Evidence Graphs offer a non-
destructive alternative grounded in the database
principle of Minimum-Cost Repair. Because inter-
mediate premises, multi-hop dependencies, and
refuting evidence spans are preserved within the
graph topology, identified defects can be resolved
by modifying the minimal sufficient subgraph nec-
essary to restore logical consistency, keeping the
original reasoning depth intact.
To demonstrate the feasibility of graph-guided
repair, we examine a stratified cohort of 100
flagged MuSiQue validation candidates spanning
factual, temporal, answer-set, and compositional
defect strata. A read-only audit of stored evi-
dence confirms 87 of these candidates as genuine
defects, resolves 8 as historical false positives, and
leaves 5 with insufficient materials for a deter-
minate verdict. Equipped with timestamped web
retrieval, the maintenance agent applies targeted
graph transformations exclusively to confirmed
defects. Across this cohort, the agent achieves
an 82.8% protocol-internal repair success rate,
successfully restoring 72.0% of all evaluated candi-
dates without manual re-annotation. The repairs
exhibit notable minimality: the agent modifies an
average of only 1.29 claim nodes per restored
graph while preserving 92.3% of verified ancestral
evidence nodes. A structural audit confirms that
98.6% of restored instances retain their original
hop count and reasoning topology, demonstrat-
ing that consistency is achieved through surgical
modifications rather than question simplification.
For non-unique queries where reference targets
were expanded into verified sets with unchanged
question text, rescoring frontier models elimi-
nates false-negative penalties, yielding a +22.4 pp
accuracy calibration across evaluated scorings.

4.5 Forward Evolution under
Boundary Control
To counter benchmark saturation caused by pre-
training contamination, forward synthesis must
satisfy two dual requirements by guaranteeing rig-
orous external factual grounding while enforcing
genuine reasoning difficulty. EverGreen achieves
this by synthesizing descendant questions directly
from verified Evidence Graphs. Because ancestral
entities, relations, and dependencies already carry
timestamped web provenance, newly added rea-
soning hops naturally inherit verifiable grounding
under controlled model failure. We evaluate for-
ward synthesis across a stratified panel of 300 seed
questions distributed evenly with 100 seeds from
each of MuSiQue, 2WikiMHQA, and HotpotQA
to reflect their source hop count and composi-
tional reasoning distributions. In this evaluation,
GPT-6-Luna acts as the examiner model guiding
the boundary-stop control policy, while DeepSeek-
Flash and Gemini-3.8-Flash are held-out answer
models assessing generalization. Guided by this
policy, the synthesis agent applies graph expan-
sions and question projection operators (entity
masking, temporal constraints, and branch join-
ing) to deepen multi-hop reasoning while preserv-
ing auditability.
To illustrate how projection operators harden
candidate questions against heuristic shortcuts
during iterative synthesis, consider an example
based on the corporate acquisition of Compaq. An
initial synthesized variant asked which company
acquired Compaq, the top personal computer
maker of 1994, and who served as its chief exec-
utive officer. Because the bridge entity Compaq
was explicitly named, models could bypass multi-
hop reasoning by directly retrieving its acquiring
company through single-hop memorization. To
prevent this shortcut, the synthesis loop applied
entity masking to rephrase the question around
the chief executive officer of the enterprise that
merged in 2002 with the vendor ranking as the
top personal computer maker in 1994. This refor-
mulation conceals the intermediate bridge entity
behind a verifiable descriptive constraint while
leaving the underlying graph dependencies intact.
Eliminating surface entity leakage thus frustrates
parametric shortcuts, forcing models to execute
genuine multi-hop deduction.
Across the 300 seeds, boundary-controlled
synthesis successfully generated 233 boundary-
reaching descendant questions, achieving an over-
all success rate of 77.7% and reaching the exam-
iner failure boundary in an average of 2.1 iterative
steps. To verify structural soundness and evidence
provenance at the atomic claim level, domain
experts audited the MuSiQue cohort, where source
decomposition graphs provide rigorous structural
baselines. Out of 96 model-screened candidates
in this cohort, expert review approved 94 items
(97.9% acceptance), with only two candidates
rejected for underspecified chronological bound-
aries.

While the closed-book accuracy cliff confirms that
synthesized descendants resist parametric recall,
a key question remains regarding whether these
hardened questions remain genuinely solvable rea-
soning tasks rather than ill-posed formulations.

Multi-hop
question
answering
benchmarks
degrade as real-world facts change and static

Evidence Graphs. Across MuSiQue, HotpotQA,
and 2WikiMultiHopQA, dependency-aware ver-
