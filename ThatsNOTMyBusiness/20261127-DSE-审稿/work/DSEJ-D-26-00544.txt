Abstract:
Side Channel Attacks (SCAs) effectively extract sensitive data from cryptographic
devices. In profiled SCAs, leakage traces for each label are extremely unbalanced
using Hamming Weight (HW) and Hamming Distance (HD) models, which results in
lacking the number of leakage traces for minority classes. To address the data
imbalance problem, this paper proposes an efficient synthetic traces scheme based on
Conditional Generative Adversarial Nets and HW (CGANHW) to generate traces for
few-sample labels. Specifically, the average leakage trace of HW model serves as the
CGAN generator's input, and the leakage traces of minority class labels are the training
dataset for the discriminator. CGANHW generates unmasked power datasets on the
ChipWhisperer (CW) platform, as well as the masked ASCAD public electromagnetic
datasets and the CHES CTF datasets. It employs Template Attacks (TA) and Deep
Learning Profiling Attacks (DLPA) to target the Advanced Encryption Standard (AES).
Experiments demonstrate that the proposed CGANHW generates both power and
electromagnetic traces. The generated traces can successfully recover the AES secret
key, outperforming those using only the original traces and other data augmentation
techniques. Additionally, CGANHW achieves comparable attack performance in 1,000
iterations on the CW dataset, compared to 3,000 iterations with CGAN.
 
Keywords: side-channel attacks, imbalanced data, conditional generative adversarial
network, Electromagnetic leakage

CGANHW: An Efficient Synthetic Trace Scheme
for Electromagnetism and Power Profiled Attacks
Abstract
Side Channel Attacks (SCAs) effectively extract sensitive data from crypto-
graphic devices. In profiled SCAs, leakage traces for each label are extremely
unbalanced using Hamming Weight (HW) and Hamming Distance (HD) mod-
els, which results in lacking the number of leakage traces for minority classes.
To address the data imbalance problem, this paper proposes an efficient syn-
thetic traces scheme based on Conditional Generative Adversarial Nets and HW
(CGANHW) to generate traces for few-sample labels. Specifically, the average
leakage trace of HW model serves as the CGAN generator’s input, and the leak-
age traces of minority class labels are the training dataset for the discriminator.
CGANHW generates unmasked power datasets on the ChipWhisperer (CW)
platform, as well as the masked ASCAD public electromagnetic datasets and
the CHES CTF datasets. It employs Template Attacks (TA) and Deep Learning
Profiling Attacks (DLPA) to target the Advanced Encryption Standard (AES).
Experiments demonstrate that the proposed CGANHW generates both power
and electromagnetic traces. The generated traces can successfully recover the
AES secret key, outperforming those using only the original traces and other data
augmentation techniques. Additionally, CGANHW achieves comparable attack
performance in 1,000 iterations on the CW dataset, compared to 3,000 iterations
with CGAN.
Keywords: side-channel attacks, imbalanced data, conditional generative adversarial
network, Electromagnetic leakage
1 Introduction
Electronic devices can leak some side-channel information about power consumption,
electromagnetic, sound, heat, etc. during the execution of cryptographic algorithms,
and the information leaked is related to the data and operation processed by the
equipment. Timing attacks (Kocher 1996) is the earliest research on side-channel
attacks (SCAs). After that, there has been a growing number of approaches to SCAs.
SCAs pose a significant threat to the security of Consumer Electronics(CE). In-depth
research on attack techniques can continuously drive the improvement of crypto-
graphic defense, thereby better protecting the security of CE (Peelam et al. 2024;
Popoola et al. 2024). Nowadays, SCAs are mainly divided into profiling attacks and

non-profiling attacks. Non-Profiling attacks require only the collection of side-channel
information on the target chip and recovering the key by means of statistical anal-
ysis. Non-Profiling attacks are differential power analysis (DPA) (Fei et al. 2012),
correlation power analysis (CPA) (Brier et al. 2004), collision attacks (Schramm et al.
2003), etc. Profiling attacks require the attacker to use the same controllable chip
as the target chip, collect the corresponding side-channel information to establish a
model between the side-channel information and the cipher, and use the model in
the subsequent attack phases. Profiling attacks include Template Attacks (TA) (Chari
et al. 2002), random models (Schindler et al. 2005), machine learning-based Profiling
attacks (Wu et al. 2023; Arpaia et al. 2023), etc. Profiling attacks rely on extensive
side-channel traces to build accurate models. However, practical constraints in trace
collection often lead to insufficient data, severely degrading attack success rates and
accuracy. In profiled side-channel analysis, Hamming Weight (HW) and Hamming Dis-
tance (HD) are widely used as leakage models. However, under uniformly distributed
plaintexts and keys, the resulting label distribution is inherently imbalanced (Picek
et al. 2019). For example, HW values 0 and 8 occur much less frequently than middle-
range values such as HW = 4, which leads to a severe shortage of minority-class traces
(Zhang et al. 2020). Ito et al. showed that such imbalance can significantly degrade
the performance of deep learning-based SCAs (Ito et al. 2021). As a result, minority
classes are insufficiently represented during profiling, limiting the model’s ability to
learn discriminative leakage characteristics and ultimately reducing attack effective-
ness. Therefore, mitigating data imbalance is essential for improving the effectiveness
of profiled SCA. In practical hardware security evaluations, addressing data imbalance
is particularly critical during the profiling phase, where evaluators construct leakage
models using open clone devices. However, capturing a statistically viable number
of minority-class traces (such as HW=0 or HW=8) requires an excessive number of
encryption executions. This makes the data acquisition process highly time-consuming
and resource-intensive. By functioning as a targeted data augmentation pre-processing
step, the proposed CGANHW framework enables evaluators to rapidly build robust
profiling models despite limited data collection budgets, ultimately supporting more
reliable vulnerability assessments of cryptographic hardware.
1.1 Traditional Synthetic Methods
Several non-generative strategies have been explored to alleviate data imbalance and
data scarcity in SCAs. One possible solution is to use the S-box output as the sensitive
intermediate value instead of the HW model, which can reduce label aggregation but
substantially increases the number of classes and makes profiling more difficult to
train effectively (Wang et al. 2022). Another line of work relies on resampling-based
balancing strategies. Picek et al. showed that such methods can partially mitigate
the negative impact of class imbalance in profiled SCA. For example, SMOTE has
been used to expand minority-class traces and improve the balance of the training set.
However, such methods do not explicitly model the underlying leakage distribution,
and excessive oversampling may increase the risk of overfitting, especially in masked
or complex leakage settings.

Interpolation-based augmentation has also been introduced into deep learning-
based SCA. In particular, Mixup was adopted to strengthen attacks under limited
training data by constructing intermediate samples between existing traces (Luo et al.
2021). Although this strategy can improve generalization, over-reliance on smoothed
intermediate samples may weaken discriminative leakage patterns and reduce attack
effectiveness. Therefore, while traditional augmentation methods can partially allevi-
ate the small-sample problem, their ability to preserve realistic leakage characteristics
remains limited.
1.2 Based-Deep Learning Synthetic Methods
In recent years, generative adversarial networks (GANs) have been widely used for
synthetic data generation (Goodfellow et al. 2014). Conditional generative adver-
sarial networks (CGANs) further incorporate label information into the generation
process, making them suitable for class-conditional sample synthesis (Mirza and
Osindero 2014). Compared with traditional resampling- and interpolation-based aug-
mentation methods, these generative approaches attempt to learn the underlying data
distribution and synthesize new traces directly.
In the context of side-channel analysis, Wang et al. introduced CGAN-based trace
generation to improve profiling attacks under limited training data (Wang et al. 2020).
That work adopted the least significant bit (LSB) as the leakage model and evaluated
both unprotected and protected implementations. However, restricting the genera-
tion process to only two labels limits its applicability to more fine-grained leakage
modeling. Another study employed HW as the leakage model and used CGAN to gen-
erate simulated power traces for deep learning-based SCAs. However, that method
was validated only on unprotected AES power traces. To further address the imbal-
anced small-sample setting, Wang et al. proposed SCA-CGAN and demonstrated that
CGAN-based augmentation can improve attack performance under data imbalance
(Wang et al. 2023).
Nevertheless, existing CGAN-based methods mainly rely on conventional noise-
driven generation and provide limited discussion on how to exploit the structural
regularity of side-channel traces or how to select the most suitable generated traces for
augmentation. These limitations motivate the present work. Different from previous
studies, the proposed method uses the mean trace as a structured conditional input
and focuses on minority-class trace synthesis under the HW leakage model.
1.3 CGANHW for Profiling Phase Data Augmentation
In this work, CGANHW is introduced as a task-specific data augmentation module
integrated into the profiling phase of side-channel analysis (SCA). The main objective
of CGANHW is to generate synthetic traces for underrepresented minority classes,
specifically those with Hamming Weight (HW) values that are sparsely represented in
the dataset (e.g., HW0, HW1, HW7, and HW8). These minority classes often suffer
from limited trace availability, making it difficult for conventional machine learning
models to learn discriminative leakage characteristics and ultimately reducing attack
performance.

The proposed method works as follows: In the profiling phase, a reference device
is used to collect side-channel traces under known plaintexts and keys. These traces
are then used to train a CGAN-based model, where the generator is conditioned on
the mean trace of the minority class and the discriminator is trained on a balanced
dataset of both original and synthetic traces. The mean trace serves as a structured
prototype, helping the generator to focus on class-dependent leakage refinement rather
than generating traces from random noise. The resulting synthetic traces are then
incorporated into the profiling dataset, improving the overall model’s ability to capture
leakage information for minority classes.
Unlike conventional augmentation methods, which rely on resampling or interpo-
lation, CGANHW directly addresses the problem of class imbalance by generating
new traces that are aligned with the leakage characteristics of the target classes. This
approach improves the robustness of the leakage model and significantly reduces the
number of attack traces required for successful key recovery in the attack phase.
Therefore, CGANHW is not just a data preprocessing technique but a critical
component of the profiling phase, specifically designed to improve the accuracy and
effectiveness of side-channel attacks under imbalanced data conditions.
1.4 Contributions
Thus, this paper first analyzes and investigates the deficiencies of data imbalance and
data insufficiency in existing synthetic methods of SCAs. It then proposes CGANHW
to generate traces for minority classes, thereby addressing these issues. Different from
previous works, this work considers the mean value of the traces as the input to the
CGAN generator and the data input to CGAN is balanced by replicating the leakage
traces. Since a device’s energy consumption depends on its operations and processed
data, it primarily comprises an operation-dependent component and a data-dependent
component. Since the same operation is executed at the determined moment, only the
data-dependent component is considered, and the data-dependent component approx-
imately satisfies a normal distribution. CGANHW can be described as follows. First,
traces containing only HW0, HW1, HW7, and HW8 are extracted to form a new
dataset. Second, this new dataset serves as the training dataset for CGAN, and the
average leakage trace of the new dataset is computed as the input to the CGAN gen-
erator to generate traces with a balanced ratio of HW0:HW1:HW7:HW8 = 1:1:1:1.
Finally, the generated traces are combined with the original traces in a 4:1 ratio to
form the dataset for SCAs. CGANHW not only efficiently reduces the degree of data
imbalance, but also increases the amount of leaked traces used for profiled SCAs. The
quality of the generated traces during CGANHW training is evaluated using Euclidean
distance and Pearson correlation coefficient. The major contributions of this paper
include as follow.
• A class-aware trace augmentation framework, named CGANHW, is developed for
profiled side-channel analysis under Hamming-weight (HW) class imbalance. By
conditioning the generation process on HW labels, CGANHW generates electromag-
netic and power traces for minority HW classes, thereby alleviating the imbalance
of the profiling dataset.

2 Preliminaries
This section first introduces the universal principle of profiled SCAs, covering both the
profiling and attack phases. It then discusses classical TA and DLPA. Additionally, a
deep learning model, GAN, capable of generating synthetic data that closely resembles
real data is introduced.
2.1 Profiled Attacks
SCAs are divided into Non-profiling attacks and profiled attacks, where profiled
attacks (Li et al. 2022) are the most powerful class of SCAs. In side-channel attacks, we
typically assume that the attacker has complete knowledge of the implementation of
the cryptographic algorithm. The attacker can repeatedly collect the physical leakage
signals generated during the operation of the target device, such as power consump-
tion, electromagnetic signals, and timing information. Additionally, the attacker has
access to plaintext-ciphertext pairs from the encryption device. The profiled attack
consists of two phases: profiling and attacking. During the profiling phase, a random
plaintext p and a random key k are used to perform m encryption rounds on a cloned
device that is identical to the target device. The leakage traces Q = {q1, q2, · · · , qm}
generated during the encryption process are collected. Based on the analysis of the
collected data, a probability density function template P[Q|λ] is estimated, where Θ

In this paper, we adopt the HW model as the leakage model, as it effectively
characterizes the energy leakage of most microcontrollers. The HW model is both
simple and easy to implement, significantly reducing computational complexity and
enhancing the efficiency of key recovery. Notably, the energy consumption associated
with data transitions from 0→1 and 1→0 is asymmetric, with the 0→1 transition
consuming more energy. Consequently, data with higher HW leads to greater energy
consumption compared to data with lower HW. Since HW exhibits a strong correlation
with actual energy consumption, its feasibility as a leakage model is experimentally
validated. As shown in Fig. 1, the average energy traces for different HW values near

2.2 Generating Adversarial Networks
GAN is an unsupervised learning deep learning model (Goodfellow et al. 2014). In
recent years, many variations of GAN have been proposed (Mirza and Osindero 2014;
Xudong et al. 2017). CGAN is one of the variants that label the samples and can
generate samples with specific labels. CGAN is a deep learning model with supervised
learning.
GAN is consisted of two models. One is the generator which aims to learn the
distribution of real samples and try to make the generated samples indistinguishable
from the real samples. The another is the discriminator whose purpose is to try to
discriminate between the real samples and the generated samples. The discriminator’s
input is the real sample and the output probability is as close to one as possible. The
discriminator’s input is the generated sample and the output probability is as close
to zero as possible. The generator and discriminator constitute a ”dynamic game”
process. Eq. (1) is the principle of the GAN.

3 CGANHW Solves the Problem of Data Imbalance
in SCAs
The following introduces CGANHW to address data imbalance and data insufficiency
issues. CGANHW uses conditional generation adversarial learning to motivate the
generator to generate side-channel traces with specific labels and close to the original
traces. The average trace is used as a CGANHW generator input to solve the label
imbalance problem by generating side channel traces with a small number of label
classes. The proposed approach is tested using the CW dataset (Luo et al. 2020),
the ASCAD dataset (Benadjila et al. 2020), and the CHES CTF 2018 dataset. Fig. 2
shows the overall flow chart of the experiment.

3.4 Mechanism Analysis of Why CGANHW Outperforms
Standard CGAN
The superiority of CGANHW over standard CGAN is attributed to the structured
conditioning strategy used for side-channel trace generation. Unlike natural images,
side-channel traces collected from the same implementation usually share a stable
global waveform determined by the underlying operation flow, while the discriminative
information is mainly reflected in local leakage variations related to the sensitive inter-
mediate value. Under this view, a trace can be interpreted as the sum of a common
operation-dependent component, a class-dependent leakage component, and residual
noise.
In standard CGAN, the generator starts from unconstrained random noise and
must simultaneously learn the global waveform morphology, the class-related leak-
age pattern, and the random disturbance. This learning problem becomes particularly
difficult when only a limited number of minority-class traces are available. In con-
trast, CGANHW uses the mean trace as a structured input, which already preserves
the dominant temporal morphology of real traces while suppressing zero-mean fluc-
tuations. Therefore, the generator mainly learns a class-guided refinement around a
realistic signal prototype rather than synthesizing the entire trace from scratch.
This design is especially beneficial for minority classes such as HW0, HW1, HW7,
and HW8. Since these classes contain only a small number of real traces, noise-driven
generation is more likely to produce unstable class-conditional distributions. By con-
trast, mean-trace conditioning provides a more informative starting point that is closer
to the manifold of realistic traces, thereby improving optimization stability and facil-
itating the preservation of leakage locations and class-related features. In addition,
balancing the discriminator-side training data reduces the bias toward majority classes
and further strengthens the modeling of minority-class traces.
Therefore, the advantage of CGANHW does not only lie in achieving better attack
results, but also in transforming trace generation from generic noise-driven synthesis
into a data-dependent and class-aware refinement process that is better aligned with
the structural characteristics of side-channel signals.

3.7 Constructing a Balanced Dataset
The HW is the most widely used power model, but it suffers from the problem of data
imbalance. According to the distribution of HW, the probability of HW being zero
and eight is only 1/256, while the probability of HW being four is 70/256. To achieve a
relatively balanced dataset (Pammu et al. 2019), the following procedures were under-
taken. Initially, traces labeled as HW0, HW1, HW7, and HW8 were extracted from
the original dataset to form the training dataset for CGAN. Subsequently, the CGAN
was trained to generate synthetic traces with a label ratio of HW0:HW1:HW7:HW8
=1:1:1:1. Finally, the original traces were combined with the generated traces in a
4:1 ratio to produce the composite dataset. The method proposed in this study effec-
tively reduced the ratio of majority to minority class labeled traces in the training
dataset from 70:1 to 4:1, significantly mitigating the degree of data imbalance. In
the experiment, the quantity of added generated traces must be carefully controlled
due to inherent differences in leakage distributions between generated and original
traces. An excessive number of generated traces may skew the model’s learned leakage
distribution towards the generated data, potentially diminishing the model’s attack
effectiveness.

4 Experiment
4.1 Experimental Setup
The PyTorch (Zimmer et al. 2021) open-source Python machine learning library is
used in this paper, and experiments are conducted using an RTX A6000 GPU to vali-
date the proposed method using four datasets. The CW dataset is generated using the
ChipWhisperer (Prouff 2014) power analysis platform developed by NewAE technol-
ogy, and executes AES encryption algorithm to collect power leakage information. The
ASCAD-fixed dataset (Benadjila et al. 2020) is a collection of electromagnetic leakage
during masked fixed-key AES encryption and decryption on ATMega8515, including
50,000 training traces and 10,000 attack traces. The ASCAD-random dataset (Benad-
jila et al. 2020) is a collection of electromagnetic leakage during masked random-key
AES encryption and decryption on ATMega8515, including 200,000 training traces
and 100,000 attack traces. The CHES CTF 2018 dataset was released at the Con-
ference on Cryptographic Hardware and Embedded Systems (CHES) in 2018 (Perin
et al. 2020). The traces were collected during the execution of a masked AES encryp-
tion on a 32-bit STM microcontroller. The CHES CTF 2018 dataset comprises a total
of 50,000 traces, with 45,000 traces allocated for the training dataset and 5,000 traces
for the attack dataset.

configured for training the CGANHW models on both the CW and ASCAD datasets

utilized as intermediate validation steps during the training phase. To more compre-

4.4.1 Generated Traces for TA
A total of 4,000 power traces were collected, among which 3,000 traces were used
for trace generation and template construction, and the remaining 1,000 traces were
used for template attacks. Since HW0, HW1, HW7, and HW8 correspond to minority
classes, traces belonging to these four classes were extracted from the 3,000 profiling
traces to train CGANHW. A total of 400 generated traces with a balanced class ratio
of HW0:HW1:HW7:HW8 = 1:1:1:1 were obtained. Based on the convergence of the
Euclidean distance and correlation metrics evaluated strictly on the training set, the
optimal generator checkpoint was identified. From this optimal state, 400 generated
traces were randomly sampled and combined with 1,600 original traces to form a
2,000-trace dataset for template construction.
To evaluate the effectiveness of the generated traces in TA, attacks were conducted
on all 16 AES-128 subkeys, and guessing entropy was adopted as the evaluation metric.
For each experiment, 50 traces were randomly selected from the 1,000 attack traces,
and the process was repeated 40 times to obtain the average guessing entropy.
Fig. 10 presents the attack results for the 16 subkeys. The orange curves cor-
respond to the template constructed with 1,600 original traces and 400 generated
traces, whereas the blue curves correspond to the template constructed with 1,600
original traces only. It can be observed that incorporating generated traces consis-
tently improves the attack performance. In particular, for Subkeys 1, 12, 13, and 14,
the number of attack traces required to achieve low guessing entropy is significantly
reduced.

achieves the best overall performance.

5 Conclusion
The proposed CGANHW framework provides a targeted solution to the data imbal-
ance problem in profiled side-channel analysis by leveraging mean-trace conditioning
and class-balanced training. This methodology integrates seamlessly with both TA
and DLPA, facilitating high-fidelity trace generation that preserves critical leakage
characteristics while significantly improving attack efficiency. Empirical evaluations
across the CW, ASCAD, and CHES CTF 2018 datasets confirm that CGANHW con-
sistently achieves superior performance in terms of guessing entropy and success rate
compared to traditional augmentation methods like SMOTE and Mixup.Subsequent
research will prioritize the framework’s generalizability across diverse cryptographic
protocols and hardware architectures. To improve robustness against highly non-linear
or non-Gaussian leakages characteristic of advanced hardware implementations, the
development of generative models capable of capturing arbitrary distribution pro-
files remains a key objective . Moreover, systematic ablation studies to disentangle
specific architectural contributions and the establishment of theoretically grounded,
adaptive sampling criteria will support the transition toward a more principled data
augmentation paradigm for hardware security assessments.

Abstract 
Side Channel Attacks (SCAs) effectively extract sensitive data from cryptographic 
devices. In profiled SCAs, leakage traces for each label are extremely unbalanced using 
Hamming Weight (HW) and Hamming Distance (HD) models, which results in lacking 
the number of leakage traces for minority classes. To address the data imbalance 
problem, this paper proposes an efficient synthetic traces scheme based on Conditional 
Generative Adversarial Nets and HW (CGANHW) to generate traces for few-sample 
labels. Specifically, the average leakage trace of HW model serves as the CGAN 
generator's input, and the leakage traces of minority class labels are the training dataset 
for the discriminator. CGANHW generates unmasked power datasets on the 
ChipWhisperer (CW) platform, as well as the masked ASCAD public electromagnetic 
datasets and the CHES CTF datasets. It employs Template Attacks (TA) and Deep 
Learning Profiling Attacks (DLPA) to target the Advanced Encryption Standard (AES). 
Experiments demonstrate that the proposed CGANHW generates both power and

key, outperforming those using only the original traces and other data augmentation
