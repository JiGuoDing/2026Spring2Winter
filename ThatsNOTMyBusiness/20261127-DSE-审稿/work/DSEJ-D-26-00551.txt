Abstract:
The topological structure of dynamic networks evolves in real time with service access
and terminal migration, and their connection behaviors exhibit strong temporal coupling
and sudden disturbance characteristics, leading to significant cross-scenario data
distribution differences. Accordingly, abnormal connection patterns present high
concealment and polymorphism. Traditional recognition methods rely on static feature
modeling for a single scenario, making it difficult to effectively extract cross-domain
shared temporal-global correlation features. In dynamic environments with sparse
abnormal samples and diverse attack types, such methods are prone to feature drift
and generalization ability attenuation. To address these issues, an intelligent
recognition method for dynamic network abnormal connection patterns based on
transfer learning with historical data is proposed. An interactive network flow dynamic
connection model is constructed, and a fine-grained connection feature matrix is
established by introducing multi-dimensional feature indicators such as the in/out
connection degrees of source/destination hosts, realizing the structured expression of
dynamic network behaviors. A stacked Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU) is introduced to
capture the evolutionary laws of connection features in the temporal dimension and
mine the temporal coupling features of abnormal behaviors. Meanwhile, a self-attention
feature enhancement module is designed to explore the global interaction
dependencies among multiple hosts, and the extracted global correlation features are
injected into the temporal model to improve the ability to identify concealed abnormal
patterns. On this basis, a transfer learning framework based on a cross-domain
transport network is built, which takes the enhanced temporal-global composite
features as input. Through bidirectional feature transfer between the historical data of
the source domain and the real-time data of the target domain, cross-scenario
migration and dynamic adaptation of abnormal recognition knowledge are achieved.
Experimental results show that the proposed method can fully cover 8 typical abnormal
attacks including unauthorized access, DDoS, and protocol tampering. In a complex
dynamic environment with the number of nodes expanded to 90, the pattern
recognition coverage remains stable above 96%, and the network throughput is
maintained above 400 Mbit/s, verifying its superiority and engineering applicability in
the task of dynamic network anomaly recognition.

Abstract: The topological structure of dynamic networks evolves in real time with service access 
and terminal migration, and their connection behaviors exhibit strong temporal coupling and 
sudden disturbance characteristics, leading to significant cross-scenario data distribution 
differences. Accordingly, abnormal connection patterns present high concealment and 
polymorphism. Traditional recognition methods rely on static feature modeling for a single 
scenario, making it difficult to effectively extract cross-domain shared temporal-global correlation 
features. In dynamic environments with sparse abnormal samples and diverse attack types, such 
methods are prone to feature drift and generalization ability attenuation. To address these issues, 
an intelligent recognition method for dynamic network abnormal connection patterns based on 
transfer learning with historical data is proposed. An interactive network flow dynamic connection 
model is constructed, and a fine-grained connection feature matrix is established by introducing 
multi-dimensional feature indicators such as the in/out connection degrees of source/destination 
hosts, realizing the structured expression of dynamic network behaviors. A stacked Gated 
Recurrent Unit (GRU) is introduced to capture the evolutionary laws of connection features in the 
temporal dimension and mine the temporal coupling features of abnormal behaviors. Meanwhile, a 
self-attention feature enhancement module is designed to explore the global interaction 
dependencies among multiple hosts, and the extracted global correlation features are injected into 
the temporal model to improve the ability to identify concealed abnormal patterns. On this basis, a 
transfer learning framework based on a cross-domain transport network is built, which takes the 
enhanced temporal-global composite features as input. Through bidirectional feature transfer 
between the historical data of the source domain and the real-time data of the target domain, 
cross-scenario migration and dynamic adaptation of abnormal recognition knowledge are achieved. 
Experimental results show that the proposed method can fully cover 8 typical abnormal attacks 
including unauthorized access, DDoS, and protocol tampering. In a complex dynamic 
environment with the number of nodes expanded to 90, the pattern recognition coverage remains 
stable above 96%, and the network throughput is maintained above 400 Mbit/s, verifying its 
superiority and engineering applicability in the task of dynamic network anomaly recognition. 
Keywords: Historical Data; Transfer Learning; Dynamic Network; Abnormal Connection Pattern; 
Intelligent Recognition; Cross-Domain Transport Network 
 
1 Introduction 
With the in-depth integration of 5G, the Internet of Things (IoT) and edge computing 
technologies, the network scale has expanded exponentially, the types of terminal access have 
become increasingly diverse, and the network topological structure and traffic characteristics have 
shown significant dynamics and heterogeneity [1,2]. Against this background, network abnormal 
connection patterns such as attack camouflage connections, cross-domain unauthorized access, 
traffic anomalies, and hybrid intrusions have become increasingly concealed and complex, and 
their manifestations continue to evolve with the iteration of business scenarios [3]. The essence of 
network abnormal connections is a sequence of behaviors that deviate from the normal 
communication paradigm [4], and the core of their recognition lies in accurately capturing the 
feature differences between normal and abnormal connections. However, in actual networks, 
normal connection patterns fluctuate dynamically with user behaviors, service loads and

a result, training data in a single scenario is difficult to cover complex and variable abnormal 
patterns, and the training of traditional recognition models based on static rule matching or single 
feature modeling is prone to overfitting [5]. As a key technology to solve the problems of data 
sparsity and domain adaptation, transfer learning mines shared knowledge among different

In response to the above challenges such as difficult cross-scenario adaptation and variable 
abnormal patterns in dynamic network anomaly recognition, scholars at home and abroad have 
carried out extensive research and proposed a variety of recognition methods based on deep 
learning and blockchain technology. Heidari et al. applied blockchain-based federated learning 
and deep learning algorithms to the detection of deepfake network attacks, a type of network 
abnormal connection [7]. This method records the identity of data contributors and data usage 
traces through encryption algorithms and distributed ledgers, uses the blockchain distributed 
ledger to record the training data features and parameter contributions of each node, and screens 
malicious nodes combined with a consensus mechanism, ensuring that data is only used for model 
training and cannot be tampered with or abused, and clarifying the ownership of data sovereignty. 
This method relies on each node having sufficient and effective abnormal samples locally to 
contribute valuable gradients. If most nodes lack key abnormal patterns, the fundamental problems 
of data imbalance and lack of representativeness cannot be solved only by a secure collaboration 
mechanism, and the model is still difficult to identify highly polymorphic new attacks. Babaei et 
al. studied the IoT network attack detection (network abnormal connection) in the fog computing 
layer based on clustering and blockchain [8]. This method encrypts and uploads the traffic features, 
clustering results, attack detection logs, model update records and other data of fog nodes to the 
chain to form an immutable distributed ledger, and screens malicious fog nodes through the 
consensus algorithm of the consortium chain; the screened fog nodes can share the effective 
features of local clustering through the blockchain to realize global feature fusion and improve the 
detection accuracy of cross-node attacks. This method fails to solve the fundamental contradiction 
in scenarios with sparse abnormal samples—if most fog nodes lack representative abnormal 
samples locally, their clustering results will mainly reflect normal patterns, and feature fusion 
based on this cannot significantly improve the ability to identify highly concealed new anomalies, 
and the problem of "generalization ability attenuation" of the model across scenarios is still 
prominent. Lilhore et al. applied the optimized CNN-LSTM and transfer learning algorithms to 
the hybrid intrusion detection of Industry 4.0 networks, a type of network abnormal connection 
detection [9]. This method automatically extracts the spatial local features of traffic data through 
the convolutional layer, captures the long-term temporal dependencies in the extracted features by 
using a gating mechanism, weights key features through an attention mechanism, and adopts a 
lightweight activation function to reduce the model complexity while ensuring detection accuracy. 
This method focuses on the optimization of a single detection model and lacks a framework 
design for continuous and online learning in a dynamic network environment, making it difficult 
to adapt to the detection requirements of real-time evolution of topology and behaviors. Turab et al. 
applied multi-modal big data representation and game theory to the security-enhanced network

2.1 Structured Representation Method for Dynamic Network Connections

Traditional static features are difficult to depict the dynamic evolutionary nature of network 
behaviors and cannot provide feature inputs with cross-domain generalization ability for

2.3 Anomaly Pattern Feature Enhancement via Global Correlation Fusion 
The temporal evolution features of dynamic network connection behaviors are extracted via a 
stacked Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU), which effectively captures the gradual progression of 
abnormal behaviors from low-frequency probing to high-frequency outbreaks. However, temporal 
features from a single-host perspective cannot fully characterize the global interaction patterns of 
multi-host collaborative attacks in dynamic networks—abnormal behaviors such as multi-source 
concurrent access in Distributed Denial of Service (DDoS) attacks, cross-host springboard 
migration in lateral penetration attacks, and botnet collaborative command response are inherently 
characterized by global correlation dependencies among multiple hosts. To address these issues, 
on the basis of temporal feature extraction, it is necessary to further introduce a global interaction 
modeling method to mine the spatial correlation features of multi-host connection behaviors, and 
inject global information into the temporal model to improve the ability to identify concealed 
collaborative abnormal patterns. 
A self-attention feature enhancement module is designed to utilize the self-attention 
mechanism to capture global correlations in connection behaviors across different hosts, uncover 
global associations in multi-host connections within dynamic networks, and improve the detection 
accuracy of anomalous patterns. The self-attention mechanism relies less on external information

2.4 History-Driven Knowledge Transfer for Dynamic Network Anomalies 
Sections 2.2 and 2.3 extract the temporal evolution features of dynamic network connection 
behaviors and the multi-host global interaction features via the Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU) and 
self-attention mechanism, respectively, forming a composite feature representation that integrates 
both temporal regularities and global correlations. However, the extraction of the above features 
and the recognition of abnormal patterns are both trained based on the historical data of the source 
domain. When applied to the real-time dynamic network scenario of the target domain, the 
recognition model trained on the source domain will face the problems of feature drift and 
generalization ability attenuation due to cross-scenario data distribution differences caused by 
factors such as the evolution of network topology, changes in service load, and migration of user 
behaviors. To address these challenges, on the basis of the temporal-global composite features, it 
is necessary to further construct a cross-domain transfer learning framework to efficiently transfer 
the anomaly recognition knowledge contained in the historical data of the source domain to the 
real-time scenario of the target domain, so as to realize the cross-scenario intelligent recognition of 
dynamic network abnormal connection patterns.

3 Experimental Analysis 
To verify the effectiveness of the proposed intelligent recognition method for dynamic 
network abnormal connection patterns, the core hardware carrier of the experimental environment 
is shown in Figure 1. The control cabinet is deployed in the core production workshop of an 
automotive parts intelligent manufacturing demonstration base (covering three process domains: 
machining, assembly and testing). The cabinet adopts a multi-level hardware architecture: the 
upper layer integrates a relay module and a signal conditioning module, the middle layer is 
equipped with a PLC control unit (including digital/analog IO modules) and a high-density 
terminal block, and the hierarchical connection of the electrical system is realized through 
yellow-green-red power cables and blue signal cables; the lower part is equipped with an 
industrial computer for equipment status monitoring and data collection. The workshop is 
simultaneously equipped with industrial Internet of Things (IIoT) terminal devices such as 
Siemens S7-1500 PLC controllers, Basler GigE Vision industrial cameras, MiR100 AGV robots, 
PT100 temperature and pressure sensors and Huawei AR502H edge gateways. The devices are 
mixed-networked through heterogeneous industrial control protocols such as Profinet (control 
layer), Modbus TCP (sensing layer), EtherNet/IP (vision layer) and OPC UA (application layer) to 
construct a dynamic industrial network topology with coexisting multi-protocols; a traffic probe 
and an intrusion detection system are deployed at the network egress, and real-time traffic 
collection is realized based on Switched Port Analyzer (SPAN) technology and connected to a 
security analysis platform. During the experiment, multi-dimensional attack vectors such as 
"external network penetration (port scanning), internal network lateral movement (protocol 
forgery), and physical layer interference (radio frequency signal injection)" are simulated to 
reproduce typical intrusion scenarios such as unauthorized access, UDP flood DDoS, and protocol 
tampering. Security operation and maintenance personnel rely on the monitoring large screen

4 Conclusion 
Aiming at the challenges such as real-time evolution of dynamic network topology, 
significant cross-scenario data distribution differences, and high concealment and polymorphism 
of abnormal patterns, an intelligent recognition method for dynamic network abnormal connection 
patterns based on transfer learning with historical data is proposed, and the following conclusions 
are drawn: 
(1) The method first constructs an interactive network flow dynamic connection model, and 
establishes a fine-grained connection feature matrix by introducing multi-dimensional feature 
indicators such as the in/out connection degrees of source/destination hosts, realizing the 
structured expression of dynamic network behaviors and providing feature inputs with 
cross-domain generalization ability for subsequent temporal modeling and transfer learning. 
(2) On the basis of structured representation, a stacked Gated Recurrent Unit is introduced to 
capture the evolutionary laws of connection features in the temporal dimension and mine the 
temporal coupling features of abnormal behaviors; meanwhile, a self-attention feature 
enhancement module is designed to explore the global interaction dependencies among multiple 
hosts, and the global correlation features are injected into the temporal model, which effectively 
improves the ability to identify concealed collaborative abnormal patterns. 
(3) A transfer learning framework based on a cross-domain transport network is further 
constructed, which takes the enhanced temporal-global composite features as input. Through 
bidirectional feature transfer between the historical data of the source domain and the real-time 
data of the target domain, cross-scenario migration and dynamic adaptation of abnormal 
recognition knowledge are achieved, solving the problems of feature drift and generalization 
ability attenuation caused by network topology evolution. 
Experimental results show that the method can fully cover 8 typical abnormal attacks 
including unauthorized access, UDP flood DDoS, protocol tampering, lateral penetration and 
signal interference. In a complex dynamic environment with the number of nodes expanded to 90, 
the pattern recognition coverage remains stable above 96%, and the network throughput is 
maintained above 400 Mbit/s, verifying its superiority and engineering applicability in the task of 
dynamic network anomaly recognition, and providing a reliable technical solution for dynamic 
network security protection in key fields such as the industrial Internet of Things.
